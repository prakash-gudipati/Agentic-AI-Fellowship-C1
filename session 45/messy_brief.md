# MealHop — raw stakeholder brief (the messy input)

> This is the kind of input you actually get: three people, three vocabularies,
> contradictions, and the same word meaning different things. Your job is NOT to
> code from this. Your job is to MODEL it first.

## From the founder (voice note, transcribed)
"So MealHop is a meal subscription thing. People sign up, pick a plan — like
3 meals a week or 5 meals a week — and we deliver. Customers can pause anytime.
The big thing is the customer never has to think — meals just show up in their
delivery window. Oh, and we do one-off orders too, not just subscriptions."

## From the kitchen lead (Slack message)
"For us an 'order' is a prep ticket — it tells the station what to cook and how
many. We batch by recipe, not by customer. We don't care who the user is, we
care what's on the ticket and when it has to be ready for the riders. Please
don't make me look up a 'subscription' — just give me tickets."

## From the ops / delivery lead (email)
"On my side an order is a stop on a rider's route. I need the recipient, the
address, and the delivery window. I don't need the menu or the price. A
'customer' to me is really just a drop-off — sometimes the person who pays
isn't even the person who receives it (gifts, family plans)."

## From the finance person (comment on a spreadsheet)
"An order is a line on an invoice. I track the subscriber as a payer with a
payment method and a billing cycle. Pauses matter to me because we don't charge
for paused weeks. I never look at recipes or routes."

## Known contradictions / traps (the founder didn't notice)
- "Customer", "user", "subscriber", "payer", "recipient", "drop-off" are used
  for what might be the SAME person — or might not (gifts, family plans).
- "Order" means four different things depending on who is talking.
- "Plan" sometimes means the subscription tier, sometimes the day's cooking plan.
- Nobody agreed on what "delivered" means (left at door? handed over? scanned?).
